    Bohr countered the challenge in the following way. The recoil imparted
on the slits by an individual particle will typically be extremely small. To
resolve this tiny change in momentum, we must know the initial momen-
tum of the screen containing the slits within a range at least as small as
the to-be-detected recoil. According to the uncertainty principle, measuring
the momentum of the screen with such high accuracy implies a large uncer-
tainty in the position of the screen. Bohr then showed that already due to
the measurement of the initial momentum of the screen (before the incident
particle even reaches the slits), the resulting indeterminacy of the position
of the screen translates into a range of possible positions of the interference
fringes on the detecting screen that would be on the order of the characteris-
tic spacing between these fringes. Thus, if we average the interference pattern
over this range of positions, the pattern is “washed out”: The position of a
