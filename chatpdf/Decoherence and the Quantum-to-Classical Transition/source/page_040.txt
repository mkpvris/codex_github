Formally, this ﬁnding is explained by the fact that quantum states represent
probability amplitudes rather than actual probabilities. This implies that a
superposition state describes a linear combination of probability amplitudes
rather than of probabilities, leading to interference terms in the probability
distribution (2.3) corresponding to the superposition. Note also that, since it
is completely feasible to carry out the double-slit experiment such that only
a single particle is present in the apparatus at any one time (see Sect. 6.2),
the interference pattern cannot be due to any interactions between diﬀerent
electrons.
