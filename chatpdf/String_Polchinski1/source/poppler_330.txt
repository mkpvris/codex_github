The resolution in the string case is the same. The momentum appearing
in the vertex operator must be at the true physical mass; this is not in
general the same as the tree-level mass, the one whose square was always a
multiple of 1/α! . Note that for certain massless fields — in particular gauge
bosons — gauge invariance guarantees that they will remain massless to
all orders. Incidentally, for massive states the mass shift is usually complex,
because these states are unstable to decay into lighter string states.
   There is a puzzle here. We saw early on that consistency of string
theory, the condition that the world-sheet theory be conformally invariant,
required the beta functions (3.7.14) to vanish. These beta functions contain
no information about higher genus surfaces — they correspond to the tree-
level string field equations. What happens when we try to expand around
a loop-corrected background? In fact things work out just right. The
dangerous limit in figure 9.8 gives rise not only to a divergence, but also
to a conformal anomaly. To see this heuristically, picture the limit as in
figure 9.3(c), as a small handle glued into the sphere. The small handle
can be represented by a series of local operators, and in particular from
the massless level there is
