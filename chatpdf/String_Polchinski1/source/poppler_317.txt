The symbols ‘∞’ and ‘8’ are supposed to depict the two sewing processes.
The parameter q is not actually needed — taking z1! = z1 q −1/2 , z2! =
z2 q −1/2 , and q ! = 1 gives an equivalent surface. It is useful to introduce q
in order to discuss the behavior as a function of q with fixed coordinate
patches.
   We can also reverse this construction. Given any Riemann surface
M, and any non-self-intersecting curve C on it, one can always find
holomorphic coordinates z1 = z2−1 in a neighborhood of C such that C
is given by |z1 | = |z2 | = |q|1/2 . Cutting on C (undoing the identification
z1 z2 = q) and gluing in the disks |z1 | < |q|1/2 and |z2 | < |q|1/2 produces
M1 and M2 (or M0 ) such that M would be reconstructed by the sewing
operation — either (9.3.3) or (9.3.4) depending on whether the cut along
C makes the surface disconnected.
