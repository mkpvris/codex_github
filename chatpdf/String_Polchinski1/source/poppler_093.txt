Such a state is conventionally known as a highest weight state. For the
CFTs of interest L0 and L̃0 are bounded below. By acting repeatedly with
lowering operators on any state we eventually reach a state annihilated
by further lowering operators, a highest weight state.
  An interesting special case is the unit operator. The operator product
T 1 is nonsingular, so it follows in any CFT that
                       Lm |1, = L̃m |1, = 0 ,        m ≥ −1 .                  (2.9.9)
As noted in section 2.6, the operators L0 and L±1 form a closed algebra,
as do L̃0 and L̃±1 . The full algebra is
                                       S L(2, C) .                            (2.9.10)
Thus |1, is also called the S L(2, C)-invariant state. It is the only such state
because the relations (2.9.7) imply that the operator A corresponding to
any S L(2, C)-invariant state is independent of position. It must then be a
c-number, as explained after eq. (2.4.24).

                                    Unitary CFTs

theory of representations of the Virasoro algebra. We will return to this
at various points, but for now we derive a few important general results

product / | , such that
