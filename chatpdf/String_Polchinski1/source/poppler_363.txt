                                A.2       Fermionic fields

Consider the basic fermionic quantum system, two states | ↑& and | ↓& with
raising operator χ̂ and lowering operator ψ̂:
                           ψ̂| ↓& = 0 , χ̂| ↓& = | ↑& ,                    (A.2.1a)
                           ψ̂| ↑& = | ↓& , χ̂| ↑& = 0 ,                    (A.2.1b)
                          {ψ̂, χ̂} = 1 , ψ̂ 2 = χ̂2 = 0 .                  (A.2.1c)
Amplitudes will be expressed as path integrals over the classical analogs
of χ̂ and ψ̂. These are anticommuting c-numbers or Grassmann variables,
elements χm and ψm of a Grassmann algebra
                     {χm , ψn } = {χm , χn } = {ψm , ψn } = 0 .             (A.2.2)
  There is a natural notion of integration over Grassmann variables, the
Berezin integral. Notice that if θ is a Grassmann variable then θ2 = 0,
and so the most general function of θ is of the form a + θb. Define
                    #                     #               #
                        dθ = 0 ,               dθ θ = −       θ dθ = 1 .    (A.2.3)

Integrals of more general expressions are determined by requiring the
integral to be linear and by defining dθ to anticommute with all Grassmann
variables. The significance of the definition (A.2.3) is that the integral of
a total derivative is zero:
                       #                    #
                              d
                          dθ (a + θb) = dθ b = 0 .                    (A.2.4)
                             dθ
For multiple Grassmann variables the integral is
                                #
                                    dθ1 . . . dθn f(θ) = c ,                (A.2.5)

where c is the coefficient of the highest term in the Taylor expansion of f,
                         f(θ) = . . . + θn θn−1 . . . θ1 c .                (A.2.6)
