                                  The disk
It is useful to construct the disk D2 from the sphere by identifying points
under a reflection. For example, identify points z and z $ such that
                                  z $ = 1/z̄ .                          (6.1.7)
In polar coordinates z = reiφ , this inverts the radius and leaves the angle
fixed, so the unit disk |z| ≤ 1 is a fundamental region for the identification.
The points on the unit circle are fixed by the reflection, so this becomes a
boundary. It is often more convenient to use the conformally equivalent
reflection
                                   z $ = z̄ .                           (6.1.8)
The upper half-plane is now a fundamental region, and the real axis is
the boundary.
  The CKG of the disk is the subgroup of P S L(2, C) that leaves the
boundary of the disk fixed. For the reflection (6.1.8) this is just the
subgroup of (6.1.6) with α, /, γ, , all real, which is P S L(2, R), the Möbius
group with real parameters. One CKV is the ordinary rotational symmetry
of the disk. Again, all metrics are equivalent — there are no moduli.

                            The projective plane
The projective plane RP2 can also be obtained as a Z2 identification of
the sphere. Identify points z and z $ with
                                 z $ = −1/z̄ .                          (6.1.9)
