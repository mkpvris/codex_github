Often the momentum integral is Gaussian, Ĥ = 12 p̂2 + V (q̂), so that the
stationary phase approximation is exact up to a normalization that can be
absorbed in the measure [dq]. In fact, we can do this generally: because the
relation (A.1.13) between p and q̇ is local, the corrections to the stationary
phase approximation are local and so can be absorbed into the definition
of [dq] without spoiling the essential property that [dq] is a product over
independent integrals at each intermediate time. The result (A.1.14) is the
Lagrangian path integral, the integral over all paths in configuration space
weighted by exp(iS /h̄), where now S is the Lagrangian action.
   We have kept h̄ so as to discuss the classical limit. As h̄ → 0, the
integral is dominated by the paths of stationary phase, δS /δq(t) = 0,
which is indeed the classical equation of motion. Henceforth, h̄ = 1.
